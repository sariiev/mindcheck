## Step 1 — Define required_points (mandatory, will be part of output)
List the specific points the answer must address to fully answer the question.
Follow these rules strictly when building this list:

- If the question names specific sub-topics (e.g. "explain X, including A and B"),
  required_points = exactly those named sub-topics. Nothing more.
- If the question is open-ended (e.g. "what is the difference between X and Y",
  "compare A and B", "explain X") AND reference material is provided: derive
  required_points ONLY from the top-level organizing points of the reference
  (e.g. its main headings or main bullets) — not from every fact, example, or
  property mentioned inside the reference. Cap this list at 5 points maximum,
  choosing the ones most central to answering the question.
- If the question is open-ended AND no reference material is provided: do NOT
  invent required_points from general/textbook knowledge of the topic. Instead
  derive required_points only from what is minimally necessary to distinguish
  X and Y at a conceptual level (e.g. for "difference between X and Y", that
  minimally means: what X is for, what Y is for, and one key structural
  difference — nothing more unless the question says more).
- A generic comparison phrasing ("difference between", "compare") is NEVER by
  itself a license to require textbook-standard dimensions (ACID, examples,
  performance benchmarks, etc.) unless the question or reference explicitly
  frames them as central to this comparison.

Example (do not deviate from this pattern):
Question: "What is the difference between OLTP and OLAP?"
Reference: a paragraph covering purpose, schema design, and consistency tradeoffs.
Correct required_points: ["purpose of OLTP vs OLAP", "schema design (normalized vs denormalized)", "consistency/redundancy tradeoff"]
Incorrect required_points: adding "ACID properties", "query types", "data freshness",
"specific product examples" — these are NOT in the reference's top-level structure
and were not asked for, so they must not appear even though they are commonly
discussed alongside this topic.
